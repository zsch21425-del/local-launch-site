28.5% of Local Pack Shops Still Don't Show Up in AI Answers

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: ranking in the Maps pack does not mean AI will name you. Saphek's Aug 7, 2026 "Get Cited by AI" piece cites a Local SEO Data study of 1,120 searches (May 2026): 28.5% of Local Pack businesses were absent from the AI answer. Top-3 pack overlap with the AI short list sat at only 48%. The same Saphek article cites Whitespark (July 2026): AI Overviews on 68% of local business-type queries, Map Pack on 39%. So a Greenville HVAC shop can sit in the three-pack and still get skipped when someone asks ChatGPT or Google's AI who to call in Mauldin.

That is the new leak. Maps rank is not the same job as AI citation.

Why the pack and the AI list diverge

AI systems do not copy the Map Pack. They pull from clean entity data: name, address, phone, services, hours, reviews that name the job, and pages that answer plain questions.

Saphek also cites Searchable (July 2026): 165 London businesses checked across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity. 93% had at least one wrong or missing fact (source). If your Simpsonville hours, service area, or phone are stale on the site or GBP, the model has nothing safe to quote.

The Stacc, July 10, 2026 shows AI Overviews on 40%+ of local services queries, organic CTR down up to 58%, and AI packs naming 1–2 businesses instead of three. CITED's GEO 2026 brief reports Pew (July 2025) numbers: traditional result clicks fall to 8% of visits when an AI summary is present, versus 15% without. In-summary links get about 1% of clicks. That same CITED brief cites Ahrefs (June 2025): AI search was 0.5% of visits but 12.1% of signups — roughly 24× the share of signups vs share of visits. Fewer clicks. Better ones. Only if you get named.

GBP completeness is still the cheap Maps lever

SerpNap's Local SEO Guide 2026 (Feb 26, 2026) weights the pack roughly like this, combining Whitespark 2025 with their own data:

  • GBP completeness ~32%
  • Review velocity and sentiment ~24%
  • On-page local relevance ~20%

SerpNap says average GBP completeness in their sample was only 41%. In the same guide they report shops that ran their full playbook saw about +156% Maps visibility and +89% direction requests in 90 days across 47 small-business clients. That is SerpNap's client sample, not a universal guarantee.

For a Fountain Inn painter or Greer plumber, that still means fill every GBP field, keep categories tight, post weekly, and ask for reviews that name the service ("duct cleaning in Travelers Rest," not just "great guy"). Review velocity beats a big stale pile.

Quick check: ask ChatGPT or Gemini for a plumber near you in Greenville. If your shop is in the Map Pack but missing from the answer, the gap is facts and quotable pages — not just star count.

What Greenville trades should fix this month

Keep it concrete:

  1. Audit your facts — phone, hours, cities served, services. Match site, GBP, and directories. Wrong NAP is how you join the 93% wrong-fact bucket Saphek cites from Searchable.
  2. Finish the GBP — photos, services, description, booking link if you have one. Completeness is still ~32% of pack weight per SerpNap.
  3. Service-named reviews — ask the week the job ends. Reply and repeat the service + city.
  4. Weekly GBP posts — finished jobs, real photos from Mauldin or Simpsonville, short seasonal notes. Not stock lawns.
  5. FAQ + LocalBusiness schema on the site — plain answers for "how much," "same-day," "areas served." AI needs quotable text, not a brochure homepage.
  6. Stop treating pack rank as the finish line — if you are in the pack and still missing from AI answers, the gap is entity accuracy and citation-ready content.

None of that needs a five-figure SEO retainer first. It needs honest data and a weekly habit.
