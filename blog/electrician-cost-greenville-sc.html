How Much Does an Electrician Cost in Greenville, SC in 2026?

Local Launch · Aug 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer: in Greenville, SC in 2026, a residential electrician charges $75–$150 for a service call, $85–$150 an hour for licensed labor, and $150–$450 for most single-fixture jobs like outlets, switches, ceiling fans, and light fixtures. Bigger work climbs fast: a panel upgrade runs $1,500–$4,000, an EV charger install starts around $450, and a full home rewiring in one of the older Greenville neighborhoods can reach $8,000–$20,000. These are rates from licensed work around Greenville County and the Upstate, not a national average.

Electrical prices here land near the national median, but a couple of local realities push them around. Greenville's older neighborhoods, think downtown, West End, and North Main, still have houses on 60-amp panels, old wired fuses, and in some cases knob-and-tube wiring. Those jobs take longer and cost more than a new-build swap. And because the Upstate runs hot and humid with a serious thunderstorm season, whole-home surge protection is a growing line item rather than an optional extra.

That's the honest number, not the "starts at $400" figure some ads use to get a truck on your street.

Why electrical work costs what it does here

  • Old panels and old wire in the historic core. Downtown Greenville, West End, and North Main have genuinely old homes, some still on 60-amp services or knob-and-tube. A simple fixture swap turns into a panel and rewiring discussion, and the estimate follows.
  • Licensed labor is a real cost. A licensed, insured SC electrician bills more than a handyman, and for panel work, grounding, or anything structural, that's who you want. Cheap work on old cable is how surge fires start.
  • The thunderstorm corridor creates surge work. Greenville catches regular severe storms, so more homeowners are asking for whole-home surge protection and SPDs at the panel. It's a growing line item and a smart one.
  • Red clay and slab foundations slow work. The heavy Upstate clay holds moisture against foundations, which complicates anything routed underground or through a slab. Running new wire in an older slab home takes longer than the same job in a crawl space.

Where the price goes up (and how to hold it down)

A few things push an electrical bill up fast: after-hours and weekend calls, old service panels that needs an upgrade before any new circuit can be added, and having to run new wire a long way from the panel. Two things pull it back down: getting a real written number instead of a verbal "probably around," and grouping smaller jobs into one visit so you're not paying the trip fee twice.

Go into the call knowing roughly where your panel is and whether you've got a 60-amp, 100-amp, or 200-amp service. That one detail shapes half the estimate on an old Greenville home, and it's written on a sticker on the panel door if you don't know.

Local reality check: the $49 or $79 "service call" ads you see around Greenville are marketing, not a price. Once a licensed electrician is on a truck, with insurance and a real license, the service call lands at $75–$150 in practice, and the repair is billed on top. Budget for the honest number.

EV chargers: the fastest-growing job

EV charging is the biggest growth area in Greenville residential electrical work right now. A basic 240-volt Level 2 wall charger install typically runs $450–$1,500 depending on how far the panel is from the garage. The charger hardware itself, $400–$1,000, is usually separate. Electricians across the Upstate are booked for these, and it's a job you genuinely want licensed, because it's a hardwired, high-amperage load on your panel.

Permits, inspections, and code reality

Most electrical work in Greenville needs a permit and a county inspection, at least for anything structural like a panel change, a new circuit, or a charger install. That inspection is not red tape for its own sake, it's the thing that makes sure a room addition doesn't end up with a 14-gauge wire carrying a 20-amp load. A licensed electrician pulls the permit, schedules the inspector, and handles the follow-up. That's part of why their rate runs higher than a handyman's, and it's money worth paying on old wiring.

On a 60-amp service, and there are still plenty in Greenville's older single-home stock, almost any real addition means the panel has to be addressed first. That's why we tell homeowners to have the panel checked before they budget for an EV charger or a big kitchen run. The charger quote you get is only real if the service can actually support it.

What not to DIY

We're all for a homeowner swapping a light fixture or replacing a dimmer switch when the wiring is dead and the breaker is off. What we won't cheer on is anyone without a license digging into the panel, running new circuits, or "fixing" a breaker that keeps tripping by swapping in a bigger one. Around here, a breaker that trips repeatedly is usually undersized wire or a worn connection, and replacing it with a larger size is how insulation melts. If a breaker trips more than once, that's an electrician's call, not a parts store run.

How to get an accurate quote

  1. Describe the job specifically, and mention what's around it (old panel, long run, slab vs. crawl space).
  2. Ask for a written estimate on the price of the work, not just the trip fee.
  3. Ask whether the service call is credited if you hire them. Many do.
  4. Get two quotes for anything over $500, which means almost any panel, charger, or rewiring job.

The bottom line: budget $75–$150 for the call, $150–$450 for most single-fixture jobs, and $1,500+ for panels, chargers, and anything touching the service. The electricians who quote honestly, show up on time, and hold a real SC license are the ones worth keeping, and they're the ones we help homeowners find.
