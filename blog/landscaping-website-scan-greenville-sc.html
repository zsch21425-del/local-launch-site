17 of 20 Greenville Landscapers Have Websites Now. Here's What Still Costs Them Calls

Local Launch · Aug 2026 · 4 min read

Short answer: only 3 of 20 landscaping companies we scanned in Greenville this month had no website at all. The other 17 had one. The interesting problems live in the details. A Wix link in a Google profile. A .net address. A hyphen nobody remembers. Those are the details that decide whether a customer finds you.

We scanned 20 landscaping businesses in Greenville in August. The old story has flipped. Almost everyone has a website now. The businesses without one stand out, and not in a good way.

But the bigger surprise was the 17 that do have sites and still make it hard to get found.

Only 3 of 20 had no website

Morales Landscaping Pine Straw, D & S Lawn Care, and Green Shadow Tree Care. No site at all. A business with no website has no pages of its own for Google to show.

The other 17 had sites. That's the new baseline. A website no longer sets you apart in Greenville. It just keeps you in the game.

The findability problems hide in the details

Three patterns stood out among the 17:

  • A Wix link where the real site should be. One company's Google Business Profile points to a Wix page. It's built fine, but it reads "template" in the first two seconds. Customers notice.
  • A .net address. Another runs on a .net domain. People type .com out of habit. If someone else owns the .com, you lose those visits before they start.
  • A hyphen nobody remembers. One company's domain has a hyphen in it. It's accurate. But who types a hyphen from memory? A customer who heard the name in passing guesses the no-hyphen version and lands somewhere else.

None of these are fatal. All of them leak calls.

Why this matters more in 2026

Google and ChatGPT now answer local searches before anyone clicks. Both work from what they can find about you: your domain, your profile, your services. A confusing link or a half-remembered address makes it easy to get left off the list.

The five-minute check

Type your business name into Google. Look at the result.

  • Does your Google Business Profile link to the site you actually want people on?
  • Is the domain the one a customer would guess, not the one you could get?
  • When an AI answers "who does landscaping in Greenville," does your name come up with the right address?

If any answer is no, that's a leak. Leaks are fixable.
