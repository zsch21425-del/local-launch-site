8 of 20 Upstate Landscapers Still Have No Website

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: on September 21, 2026 we pulled 20 landscaping businesses from a Greenville, SC Maps query. Twelve had websites. Eight did not. The result set skewed into the Spartanburg cluster, not a pure downtown Greenville list. That still matters. Homeowners searching “landscaping near me” across the Upstate land on that pack. Eight crews with real phones and a few reviews still have no page Google can rank.

That is a bigger no-site gap than our August Greenville-only landscaping scan, where 17 of 20 already had a site and only three had none. Core Greenville page one is tighter. Off the core, the no-site pool is still open.

What the September 21 pull actually showed

We used the same free research stack we use for daily SEO/GEO work. Titles and Maps fields only. No invented conversion rates.

Has-site leaders from the same pull:

  • Veterans Landscaping — veteranslandscapingsc.net — 4.7 stars from 172 reviews
  • Landscape Innovations — landscapeinnovationssc.com — 4.6 stars from 74 reviews

Those two look like businesses customers already trust. Reviews, a real domain, and a site Google can crawl.

No-site names that showed up in the same 20 (examples, not a full directory): K.B. Landscaping, Thompson Landscaping, Tumax, Contractless Lawn, Thomas Grading, Green Shadow Tree Care. Some have a handful of reviews. None had a website in that pull. A Google Business Profile with a phone is not the same as a page that answers “do you do mulch in Mauldin?” or “hardscape in Simpsonville?”

Why this still costs jobs in 2026

The 2026 service-business design guides keep saying the same thing. Impact Marketing Solutions (July 8, 2026), Forget Me Never Media’s six things every local service site needs, plus local-service design write-ups from Shaheer Malik, WisdomStream, and To The Max Media all frame the site as a conversion system, not a brochure.

For a landscaper that means:

  • A sticky call button on mobile.
  • A short form someone can finish in a driveway.
  • Trust in plain view — reviews, service area, real job photos.
  • Service + city language a searcher and an AI answer can quote.

No site means none of that. The homeowner in Fountain Inn picks the crew that shows up with a page and 70+ reviews. The no-site crew never enters the short list.

Local note: page-one Greenville pressure washing and HVAC are often fully sited. Landscaping off the core Greenville pack still leaves real no-site gaps. Do not treat every trade the same.

What to do if you are one of the eight

  1. Claim and complete the Google Business Profile first if it is thin.
  2. Put a real site live with services, cities you cover, phone, and a short form.
  3. Ask every finished job for a review that names the work and the town.
  4. Post on the profile weekly. Fresh photos beat a dead wall.
  5. Only then add extras (AI receptionist, heavy ad spend). Site and profile first.

If you already have a modern site and strong reviews, the gap is usually findability and follow-up, not another redesign. If you have no site, or a 2016 template nobody opens, fix that before you buy more tools.
