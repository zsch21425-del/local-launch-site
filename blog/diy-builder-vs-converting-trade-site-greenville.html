DIY Builders Cost $17–$50 a Month. Most Service Sites Still Convert at 1–2%

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: a DIY builder can look cheap and still lose jobs. Surmado (Aug 5, 2026) puts DIY website builders at about $17–$50 a month. Foundgrove (May 12, 2026) puts typical service-business sites at only 1–2% conversion, while Unbounce’s landing-page median sits near 6.6%. Impact Marketing Solutions (Jul 8, 2026), citing eDesign Interactive’s look at 200+ plumber, HVAC, electrician, and roofer sites, still shows load under 2 seconds near 32% conversion, 3–5 seconds near 18%, and over 5 seconds near 6%. Single-field “Get a quote” forms land around 12%. Three fields fall to about 4%. Five or more fields land at 1–2%.

So the monthly fee is not the real bill. The slow page and long form are.

Cheap monthly does not mean cheap lead cost

A Mauldin landscaper on a $29/mo template still pays when a Fountain Inn homeowner bounces after three seconds. PERC Engage (Mar 9, 2026) puts about 60% of local service searches on mobile, says click-to-call lifts conversion about ~28%, and reports 53% leave after a load longer than 3 seconds. Mobile forms with four fields or fewer convert about 160% better.

If the phone number is buried, the form asks for a novel, and the first screen crawls on LTE in Greer traffic, the $17–$50 “savings” is theater.

What Greenville Maps already show

Having a domain is common now. Our September 10, 2026 Maps pull for HVAC in Greenville returned 0 of 20 with no website. Our September 7 landscaping pull still found 4 of 20 with no site (about 20%), including shops around Easley. Most ranked peers already have live .com or .site domains.

In trades where everyone has a site, the DIY vs custom fight is not “online or not.” It is who gets the call.

What national pricing still looks like

2026 guides keep professional builds high:

  • Surmado (Aug 5, 2026): freelancers $1,500–$8,000 one-time; agencies $6,000–$35,000+; managed web presence $99–$500/mo; most professional builds $3,000–$10,000
  • Project Cost Estimator (Apr 2026, 600+ quotes): median freelancer $3,000–$5,000; agency $5,000–$25,000
  • Jim.com (Aug 18, 2026): most professional SMB builds $3,000–$15,000; ongoing owner costs $1,100–$5,000/yr
  • Garrett Handley (Sep 3, 2026): Elementor/GoDaddy-band freelancers $2k–$8k; agencies $10k–$35k+; GoDaddy templated design from $499 one-time

A South Carolina agency we verified in August 2026 listed $1,750 to build, $550–725/mo for maps, and $950/mo for SEO.

You do not need a five-figure brochure to beat a weak Wix page. You need a fast page, a short path to call, and a Google profile that stays current.

The boring stack that actually converts

  1. Under ~2 seconds on mobile cellular — that is the conversion cliff in the trade-site data.
  2. Sticky click-to-call — one number a thumb can hit without hunting.
  3. Short quote path — prefer one field; keep it to four or fewer.
  4. Real job photos from Greenville, Mauldin, Simpsonville, Greer, Anderson, Easley — not stock lawns from another state.
  5. Service + city in plain text so Google and AI answers can name what you do.
  6. Weekly Google Business posts + review replies — a live domain with a dead profile still loses.

DIY tools can ship some of that. Most owners never finish the setup. The template looks done. The conversion work is not.

Quick test: open your site on cellular. If the phone number is not tappable in about two seconds, the $29/mo builder fee is not the problem.
