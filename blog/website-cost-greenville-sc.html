How Much Does a Website Cost in Greenville, SC in 2026?

Local Launch · Aug 2026 · 5 min read

Direct answer: a professional, modern website for a Greenville, SC small business costs $300–$350 for a high-converting single-page site, and roughly $500–$1,500 for a multi-page build with blog, galleries, or e-commerce. Most local small businesses, plumbers, landscapers, mechanics, pay $300–$500 and get everything they need: a fast site, mobile design, Google setup, and a call-to-action that generates leads.

Those are real numbers from actual Upstate SC projects, not national averages. The internet is full of "$2,000–$10,000 website" figures from agencies serving bigger markets with bigger overhead. In Greenville, the market is different, and so is the honest price.

Why the range is so wide

Three things drive the price up or down:

  • Pages. A one-page site for a plumber with 4 services is cheap. A 15-page site for a dentist with service pages, team bios, and a blog costs more. Every page is content that has to be written and structured.
  • Features. A contact form is standard. A photo gallery, an AI visualizer that shows customers their patio before it's built, a review feed, or a booking calendar each add time, and value.
  • Who builds it. A local freelancer or small agency in Greenville charges a fraction of a national firm with a sales team and account managers. You're paying for the work, not the overhead.

Where the hidden costs live

The build is one thing. What most business owners forget:

  • Domain and hosting, $15–$30/year, often bundled.
  • Ongoing maintenance: sites need updates, security checks, and content refreshes. Plan $35–$50/month or pay per change.
  • SEO: a beautiful site nobody can find isn't a business asset. If the site isn't structured for local search from day one, you'll pay to fix it later.
  • Photos and copy: a site is only as good as its content. Real photos beat stock every time.

Greenville-specific note: in the Upstate, most service businesses win with a single high-quality page plus a strong Google Business Profile, not a sprawling site. Start lean, measure calls, add pages as they pay for themselves.

How to get an accurate quote

  1. List your 3 most profitable services, those get featured placement.
  2. Write down how customers find you today (Google, referrals, Facebook).
  3. Ask any builder: "What does a one-page site cost, what's included, and what's monthly?"
  4. Get it in writing. A good local builder can quote a single-page site in one conversation.

The bottom line: in Greenville in 2026, a working, professional website is a $300–$500 decision for most small businesses. Anything charging $2,000+ for a basic site is selling you overhead, and you can do better locally.
