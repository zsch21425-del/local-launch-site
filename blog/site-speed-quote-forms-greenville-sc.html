A Site That Loads in Under 2 Seconds Converts at 32%. Over 5 Seconds Drops to 6%.

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: on service-business sites, load time and form length still decide who gets the job. Impact Marketing Solutions (July 8, 2026), citing eDesign Interactive's look at 200+ plumber, HVAC, electrician, and roofer sites, puts conversion at about 32% when the page loads under 2 seconds, 18% at 3–5 seconds, and 6% past 5 seconds. They also report a roughly 2-second speed gain can lift lead volume 50%+. Single-field “Get a quote” forms convert around 12%. Three fields fall to about 4%. Five or more fields land at 1–2%. To The Max Media (May 2026) adds that 73% of local-intent searches are mobile, and the 2026 80/20 is a sticky call button plus a book-a-call path inside about 90 seconds.

That is not abstract web theory. A Mauldin HVAC owner whose Wix page crawls on LTE loses the same lead a fast Greenville competitor keeps. A Simpsonville plumber who asks for name, email, phone, address, and “describe your issue” before anyone can tap Call is filtering out tired homeowners on purpose.

What the numbers mean on a phone in Greenville

Most of the search happens in the truck line, the driveway, or the kitchen after work. Someone in Fountain Inn types “AC not cooling near me.” Someone in Greer wants a lawn quote before the weekend. Someone in Easley needs a pine-straw drop this week.

If the first screen takes five seconds to paint, a lot of those people bounce. If the first screen paints fast but the only action is a long form buried under stock photos, they bounce too. The research above is from trade-site samples and local-intent mobile data — not a national e-commerce study dressed up for contractors.

Form friction is the quiet leak

Owners often think more fields mean better leads. The eDesign-backed numbers say the opposite for service quotes:

Form shapeReported conversion
Single-field “Get a quote”~12%
3 fields~4%
5+ fields~1–2%

For a Greenville trade, the first job of the homepage is the call or a one-line callback request. Name + phone is enough to start. Address and job notes can wait for the conversation. A sticky tap-to-call bar on mobile is not fancy. It is how people actually hire.

Speed is not a redesign hobby

You do not need a brand-new identity to fix a slow site. You need fewer heavy scripts, compressed photos of real jobs, and a page that works on a mid-range Android in Travelers Rest traffic. Impact Marketing’s cited split — 32% under 2s vs 6% over 5s — is the business case. A ~2-second improvement tied to 50%+ more leads is why “it looks fine on my office Wi-Fi” is a bad test.

Check your own page on a phone with cellular data. Time how long it takes before the phone number is tappable. If that is past a couple of seconds, fix speed before you buy more ads.

A short checklist for Upstate service sites

  1. Homepage loads under ~2 seconds on mobile cellular.
  2. Phone number is visible and tappable without scrolling on a phone.
  3. Sticky call / book path within the first ~90 seconds of a visit.
  4. Quote form is one field when you can, three at most.
  5. Real job photos from Greenville, Mauldin, Simpsonville, or Anderson — not stock lawns from another state.
  6. Service + city named in plain text so Google and AI answers can read what you do.

Bottom line

Under 2 seconds and a short path to call still beat a pretty slow brochure. The July 2026 trade-site numbers (32% vs 6% by load time, 12% vs 1–2% by form length) and the May 2026 mobile local-intent figure (73%) are the reason. If your Greenville or Easley site takes forever and asks for a novel before a callback, you are paying for traffic that never becomes a job.

Fix the load. Shorten the form. Put the phone where a thumb can hit it.
