Most Service Sites Take 42 Hours to Answer a Lead. 23% Never Reply

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: the form is not the finish line. Foundgrove (May 12, 2026) says a typical service-business site converts only 1–2% of visitors into leads. The same piece cites research putting average web-lead response at 42 hours, with 23% never responding at all. Unbounce’s landing-page median sits near 6.6%. So even when a Greenville homeowner fills the form, almost a day and a half can pass before anyone calls back — if anyone does.

That is the quiet leak. Your Maps rivals already have websites. Speed to the phone is what still separates shops.

42 hours is how you lose a driveway job

A Mauldin homeowner who needs the driveway cleaned before guests arrive will not wait two days. They hit the next shop on the list. Or they call the number still sticky on their phone from last year.

Foundgrove’s stack for closing the 1–2% gap is not “prettier design.” It is Core Web Vitals, a short form, and human trust signals (reviews, real photos, clear contact). Then someone has to pick up the lead.

If your site loads fine but the inbox sits overnight, you paid for traffic you will not close.

Quick test: submit a test lead on your own form at 9am. If nobody calls or texts back the same morning, the site is not the whole problem.

What “fast response” looks like on a trade site

Keep it boring and local:

  1. Sticky click-to-call on mobile — thumb hits the number without scrolling a novel.
  2. Short form — name, phone, one job note. Foundgrove and the trade-site conversion numbers both punish long forms.
  3. Same-day reply rule — under an hour is better; same morning beats “we’ll get back to you.”
  4. Text or call preference noted on the form so the first reply matches how the customer wants to talk.
  5. After-hours coverage — voicemail alone still loses. An AI receptionist or a shared on-call phone stops the 23% “never reply” bucket from eating jobs in Greer and Fountain Inn at 8pm.

None of that needs a five-figure redesign. It needs a path from form submit to a human voice.
