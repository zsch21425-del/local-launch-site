40% of Booking Enquiries Hit After Hours. DIY Listing Tools Won't Catch Them Alone

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: about 40% of booking enquiries land outside business hours. That figure comes from DataLatte's 2026 booking-agent page (checked September 2026). Their page also describes website chat, Google Business Profile chat, and SMS coverage, with missed-call text-backs in about 90 seconds. Getpin claims half of local listings are outdated, and that 70–80% of people research a business online before they reach out. For a Greenville HVAC tech or a Mauldin plumber, nights and weekends are still real job volume. A quiet phone after 5pm is not "off the clock." It is unpaid lead loss.

What after-hours actually looks like in the Upstate

A homeowner in Simpsonville Googles "AC not cooling" at 8:40pm. Someone in Fountain Inn wants a pressure-wash quote on Saturday morning. Greer and Travelers Rest run the same pattern after shift change.

If your site only has a contact form and your Google profile never posts, those people move on. As of September 2026, DataLatte prices full booking-agent builds at $800–$2,000 plus $40–$90 a month. Ask-Zenna lists a done-for-you AI receptionist Pro plan at $97 a month with no setup fee (same snapshot). The market already treats after-hours capture as normal gear, not a luxury add-on.

DIY listing tools fill one gap. Not all of them.

Getpin AI sells maps sync, AI Google posts, and review ask/reply across Google, Apple, and Bing. As of September 2026 their tiers run $45 / $85 / $150 a month. They claim about a 30% traffic lift in roughly two months for many users. That is a DIY login, not a person watching your profile.

GMB Briefcase (September 2026 pricing page) packs rank grids, AI post scheduling, and review tools. It also adds social, chatbot, site builder, and an LLM tracker for ChatGPT and Perplexity — $2 per Google listing. GrowthPro (as of July 2026) sells "autonomous" agents. They run weekly GBP posts, review replies, and rank checks. They also watch AI Overview visibility, citation/NAP cleanup, and GEO schema freshness around the clock.

Useful tools. Still tools. Someone has to set the service area. Someone has to write honest posts. Someone has to fix a wrong NAP line in Greenville vs Spartanburg. And someone has to answer when a real lead texts back at 9:12pm.

A simple Greenville checklist

  1. Answer after hours somehow — missed-call text, chat, or a receptionist that knows your services and cities.
  2. Keep Google, Apple, and Bing listings in sync with the same name, phone, and address.
  3. Post on Google Business Profile weekly. One real job photo from Anderson or Easley beats a generic stock graphic.
  4. Ask for reviews that name the service and the city, not "great guys!"
  5. Put plain service pages on the site so Google and ChatGPT can read what you actually do.

Bottom line

Forty percent of booking enquiries, per DataLatte's figures, show up when the truck is parked. Half of local listings may be stale while most buyers research online first — that is Getpin's claim, not a third-party study we re-ran. DIY software can post and sync. It will not replace a clean site, weekly profile work, and a real path to catch the 8pm call in Greenville.

If your profile is quiet after dinner, fix that before you buy another logo.
