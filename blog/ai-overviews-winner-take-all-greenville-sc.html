AI Overviews Hit 58% of Local Service Searches. The Pack Now Names 1–2 Shops

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: Google's AI Overviews now show on 58% of local services queries. That is up from 41% a year earlier, per The Stacc's July 10, 2026 look at an 84,000-query tracker. The same Stacc report says AI local packs name 1–2 businesses — about 68% fewer slots than the old three-pack — and that top organic results can lose up to 58% CTR when an Overview is on the page. Businesses cited inside the Overview pick up about +35% organic CTR (and +91% on paid), still per Stacc. BrightLocal 2026 still puts 45% of consumers using AI for local recommendations (was 6%). For a Greenville HVAC tech or Simpsonville painter, ranking "somewhere on page one" is not the game anymore. Getting named in the short list is.

Most Upstate shops still post to Google once a quarter and hope. That is how you stay off the short list.

What changed for local service search

The classic map pack gave you three names. AI packs shrink that. One or two shops get the citation. Everyone else fights for scraps under the Overview, or gets zero-clicked on mobile.

Emulent's April 21, 2026 Local SEO projection still puts GBP signals around 32% of Local Pack weight and reviews around 16–20%. Only 22–30% of local businesses actively manage GBP with weekly updates. Those that do earn about 2–4× better pack position. Complete LocalBusiness schema shows up on only 12–18% of local sites, and Emulent ties that completeness to roughly 35% higher CTR. That same Emulent projection puts mobile local zero-click near 78%.

So the winners are not always the shops with the prettiest brochure site. They are the ones Google and AI systems can name with a clean profile, fresh reviews, and plain answers on the page.

Review velocity beats a stale pile

ECOSIRE (June 6, 2026) still holds that ~80 reviews growing about 10 a month routinely beats 300 stale ones. The Stacc's local visibility checklist aims for 5+ new reviews a month, 100% replies within 48 hours, and at least 4 GBP posts a month. Lyxel & Flamingo's June 26, 2026 GEO playbook puts AI Overviews on about 68% of local searches (92–97% on informational/hybrid queries), notes ChatGPT at 900M weekly active users, and stresses that JSON-LD alone does not lift ChatGPT citations. You still need quotable Q&A, a clear local entity, and third-party proof.

For a Mauldin landscaper, that means ask for a review the week the job finishes. Name the service in the reply ("Thanks for trusting us with the pine straw install in Greer"). Post four short GBP updates a month — finished jobs, seasonal tips, real photos from Fountain Inn or Travelers Rest. Not generic stock lawns.

What Greenville trades should fix this month

  1. GBP description in plain English — lead with "We [service] in [City, SC]." Not a slogan paragraph.
  2. Weekly GBP posts — four or more a month. Job photos from Greenville, Mauldin, Simpsonville, Anderson, Easley.
  3. Review requests every closed job — aim for 5+ new reviews a month with the service named in the text.
  4. Reply to every review in 48 hours — short, keyword-honest, human.
  5. LocalBusiness + FAQ on the site — most local sites still skip schema (only ~12–18% complete it). Put real Q&A on the page so AI answers can quote you.
  6. One clear phone path on mobile — zero-click is high; the people who do click still need a tappable number.

Quick test: open your Google profile. If the last post is older than a week and your last five reviews never name the job, you are not playing the short-list game yet.
