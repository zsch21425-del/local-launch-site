Most Service Searches Are on a Phone. Here's What Greenville Sites Still Miss

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: about 73–75% of service searches happen on mobile. Every extra second of load time costs roughly 7% of conversions. Sticky Call and Quote buttons plus fresh reviews beat fancy parallax every time. In Greenville, that gap is real — we pulled 20 local pressure washers on Maps and every single one already has a website. The winners stack reviews, keep a phone button in reach, and still leave LocalBusiness schema half-done.

Having a site is table stakes now. Speed and trust decide who gets the call.

What the 2026 guides actually say

Impact Marketing (July 2026) and To The Max Media (May 2026) both put service search traffic in the low-to-mid 70s percent on phones. Clifton Designs' 2026 service-site trends list the same boring stack: fast mobile pages, sticky CTAs, reviews up front, LocalBusiness schema, and city or service-area pages.

None of that is about looking "premium." It is about a homeowner in Mauldin who needs a driveway washed today and will tap the first number that loads clean.

Greenville proof: pressure washing is fully on the web

On September 4 we sampled pressure washing businesses in Greenville, SC on Google Maps (monid Apify scrape). Result: 20 out of 20 had websites.

Leaders sat around 60–144 reviews at 4.9–5.0 stars. Common on the better sites: sticky phone links and review widgets. Common gap: LocalBusiness schema was inconsistent. Some stacks were WordPress. Others mixed Wix and job software.

So the "we need a website" pitch alone is weak in that trade. The real question is whether the site is fast on a phone, easy to call, and clear enough for Google and AI to cite.

Four fixes that still move the needle

  1. Sub-2-second feel on mobile. Heavy hero videos and parallax look nice on a desktop. On LTE in Simpsonville they cost taps. Compress media. Keep the first screen light.
  2. Sticky Call / Get Quote. Thumb reach beats a buried contact form. Leaders already do this.
  3. Reviews where people land. A star row near the top beats a testimonials page nobody opens. Recency matters as much as count.
  4. LocalBusiness schema + real service pages. Name, phone, city, and services in structured data. Separate pages for what you actually do in Greenville, Greer, or Fountain Inn — not one thin homepage.

Skip the agency theater. Ship the boring four.
