46% of Searches Have Local Intent. Does Google AI Cite Your Greenville Business?

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: about 46% of searches have local intent in 2026. Nearly half the time someone types into Google, they're looking for a business near them — a plumber in Greenville, a landscaper in Mauldin, a pressure washer in Simpsonville. AI Overviews now answer most of those local queries before anyone clicks a link. Ranking alone is not enough. Getting cited in the answer is the job.

Local demand is still the core channel for trades. The way people see the answer changed.

What "local intent" actually means for an Upstate trade

When someone searches "HVAC repair near me" or "roof leak Greenville SC," they are not browsing. They need a name, a phone number, and a reason to call. Google used to show a list of blue links. Now it often writes the answer first — AI Overviews and AI Mode pull a short list of businesses and facts from the same index Google has always used.

Search Engine Journal, Digital Information World, and TheStacc all covered the same 2026 pattern: AI Overviews answer most local searches. If your shop is not in that answer, the customer may never open your site.

QuickSEO's May 2026 local-SEO roundup put the local-intent share at 46%. For a Greenville-area trade, that is not a vanity stat. It is most of your demand.

Why citation beats a bare ranking

You can still rank on page one and lose the call. The overview sits above the organic list. It names 2–4 businesses, pulls hours and reviews, and ends the search for a lot of people.

What gets you named:

  • A complete Google Business Profile (categories, service area, hours, photos, recent reviews)
  • A fast site Google can index — real service pages, not a thin homepage
  • Content that answers real local questions ("what does a water heater replacement cost in the Upstate," "does this company do retaining walls in Greer")
  • Consistent name, address, and phone across the web

That package is what people now call GEO — generative engine optimization. RankOps and Adaptify both treat it as first-class local work in 2026. Google's own docs still frame it as SEO fundamentals. Either way, the work is the same: give AI something solid to cite.

What Greenville shops usually skip

Most Upstate trades we talk to have a Facebook page and a half-finished Google profile. Some have a Wix site with no service pages. Almost none have written answers to the questions customers actually ask AI.

A landscaper in Fountain Inn does not need a 20-page brochure site. They need:

  1. A clean site with services, service area, and a phone CTA
  2. A Google profile that gets a new photo or post most weeks
  3. A steady drip of real reviews
  4. One or two pages that answer local cost and process questions in plain English

Do those four and Maps plus AI Overviews have something real to cite.
