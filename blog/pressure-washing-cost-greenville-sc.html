How Much Does Pressure Washing Cost in Greenville, SC in 2026?

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer: in Greenville, SC in 2026, pressure washing usually runs $100–$300 for a driveway, $250–$550 for a single-story house wash, and $350–$700 for a two-story exterior. Soft wash on siding or a roof often lands $150–$400 for smaller homes and $400–$800 when the surface is heavy with algae. Most Upstate homeowners pay $200–$500 for a typical residential visit. Those ranges come from Greenville-area quotes and local company guides, not a national average pulled from a spreadsheet.

The Upstate has its own dirt problem. Red clay stains concrete. Pine pollen coats everything every spring. Humidity grows black algae on vinyl and brick faster than dry climates do. That is why a "simple wash" here often needs more dwell time, more chemistry, and more rinse water than the same job would in a cooler market.

Why Greenville pressure washing costs what it does

  • Red clay. Driveways in Simpsonville, Mauldin, and Greer pick up a pink-red film that plain water will not move. Crews use surface cleaners and the right detergent, or the stain comes back with the next rain.
  • Algae and humidity. Black streaks on vinyl and brick are living growth, not dirt. Soft wash chemistry kills it. High pressure alone can blast siding and still leave the organism alive.
  • Pollen season. Every spring the Upstate gets a thick yellow coat. Homes washed in March often need a light follow-up by May if nobody rinsed the pollen film off.
  • Two-story access. A lot of Greenville and Travelers Rest stock is two story. Ladders, longer hose runs, and careful roof-line work add real time.

Soft wash vs. straight pressure

Homeowners still say "pressure wash" for everything. Pros here split the work. Concrete driveways and sidewalks take higher pressure with a surface cleaner. Vinyl, fiber cement, painted wood, and roofs usually get a soft wash: low pressure, detergent dwell, then a rinse. That split matters for price and for damage. A cheap high-pressure pass on old vinyl can force water behind the boards and void a siding warranty. A good Greenville crew will tell you which method they are using before they spray.

Where the price goes up (and how to keep it down)

Three things inflate a quote fast: heavy organic growth that needs a second chemical pass, oil or rust stains on concrete, and second-story work with tight side yards. HOA communities around Five Forks and Fountain Inn also care about runoff and plant protection, which means more pre-wetting and more rinse time.

Bundling keeps the number sane. House plus driveway in one visit almost always beats two separate trips. Spring and early fall book up first around here, so shoulder-season dates can land a better rate if your schedule is flexible.

Local reality check: the "$99 whole house" ads you see around Greenville are usually a teaser for a tiny footprint, water-only rinse, or a loss leader that pads with add-ons on site. A real single-story soft wash with proper dwell time still lands closer to $250–$500. Budget for the honest number, not the ad.

How to get an accurate quote

  1. Send a few phone photos: front, both sides, driveway, and any stained spots.
  2. Ask whether the house portion is soft wash or high pressure, and what cleaner they use on algae.
  3. Get a flat total for the surfaces you want cleaned, not an hourly guess.
  4. Ask what is protected (plants, outlets, windows) and whether they rinse landscaping after.
  5. Get two written quotes on anything over $400, and ask for recent local job photos.

DIY vs. hiring it out

A small patio or a short walkway is fair DIY if you already own a decent machine and know how far to stand off the surface. A full house is a different job. Rental machines from big-box stores around Greenville run roughly $40–$100 a day, plus detergent, plus your Saturday. The risk is the finish: etched concrete, forced water in siding, or algae that returns in weeks because nothing killed the growth. For a two-story exterior or a driveway full of clay and tire marks, a crew with a surface cleaner and soft-wash gear usually finishes cleaner and faster than a weekend fight with a rental wand.

When to wash in the Upstate

Late spring after the heavy pollen drop, and early fall before leaf stain sets into concrete, are the sweet spots. Mid-summer works too, but crews are busier and afternoon storms can pause a job. Roof soft wash is worth timing before peak summer heat if black streaks are already obvious from the street. Most homes here look sharp with a full exterior wash once a year and a driveway touch-up when clay or oil starts to show.

The bottom line: budget $100–$300 for driveways and $250–$700 for house work in Greenville, with packages often the better buy.
