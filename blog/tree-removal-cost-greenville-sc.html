How Much Does Tree Removal Cost in Greenville, SC in 2026?

Local Launch · Sep 2026 · 6 min read

Direct answer: in Greenville, SC in 2026, tree removal usually runs $250–$700 for a small tree, $700–$1,800 for a medium tree, and $1,800–$4,500 for a large one. Complex jobs near a house, power line, or steep lot can hit $4,000–$9,000+. Most Upstate homeowners land between $500 and $2,500 for a typical residential take-down with haul-away. Those bands come from Greenville-area company guides and local quote ranges, not a national average pasted from a calculator.

The Upstate grows big trees fast. Pines, oaks, sweetgums, and water oaks fill lots from Simpsonville to Travelers Rest. Soft ground after heavy rain, red clay that turns to slick mud, and storm seasons that drop limbs on roofs all change what a “simple” removal costs here. Height alone never tells the whole story.

Why Greenville tree work costs what it does

  • Tall pines and tight lots. Many Greer, Mauldin, and Five Forks yards put a mature pine between the house and the neighbor fence. Crews climb and cut in sections instead of dropping the whole trunk. That is slower, safer, and more expensive.
  • Red clay and wet ground. After a week of rain, equipment sinks. Bucket trucks and chippers need firmer access, or the crew works by hand and rope. Soft soil adds hours.
  • Storm season. Summer storms and leftover ice damage mean more emergency calls. If a tree is on a roof in Pelzer or Easley at 9 p.m., you are not paying the weekday open-yard rate.
  • Insurance and equipment. A real tree service carries liability and workers’ comp, plus chippers, ropes, and sometimes a crane. The low cash quote with no paperwork is usually the expensive one later.

What drives a quote up or down

Four things move the number most: height, lean, distance to structures, and access. A 40-foot oak in an open Fountain Inn yard is one job. The same oak over a carport on a narrow West Greenville lot is another. Dead wood can be lighter to cut. It is also more dangerous to climb, so some crews price it higher, not lower.

Stump grinding is the common surprise. Many “tree removal” ads cover the trunk and canopy only. Grinding the stump, hauling chips, and seeding the hole often add $100–$400. Ask for a line-item total so you are not comparing apples to stumps.

Local reality check: the “$99 tree removal” ads around Greenville are usually a sapling, a bush, or a teaser that balloons once the crew sees power lines or a fence. A real medium pine with haul-away still lands closer to $700–$1,800. Budget for the honest number, not the ad.

How to get an accurate quote

  1. Send clear phone photos: full tree, base, lean direction, and anything under it (roof, car, fence, wires).
  2. Ask whether stump grinding and haul-away are included, and get that in writing.
  3. Confirm they are insured in South Carolina and ask for a certificate if the tree is near your house.
  4. Get a flat project total, not a vague hourly guess that can run all day.
  5. On anything over $1,500, get two written quotes and recent local job photos.

DIY vs. hiring it out

A small, dead ornamental tree you can reach from the ground with a handsaw is fair DIY. You need the tools and a clear fall path. Anything that needs a ladder is a different story. Homeowners get hurt every year in the Upstate trying to notch a leaning pine. Rental chainsaws and a Saturday do not replace ropes, a spotter, and a chipper.

If the tree is taller than your single-story roof, near a structure, or near utility lines, hire a crew. A crushed fence or a hole in a metal roof wipes out the savings. For storm wood hanging over a driveway in Greenville or Simpsonville, call a pro the same day.

When to remove a tree in the Upstate

Late fall through early spring is the easier window for planned work. Leaves are down. Ground is often firmer between storms. Crews are less slammed than after a July thunderstorm. Summer still works, but heat and afternoon storms slow jobs. If a tree is already dead, hollow, or leaning toward the house, do not wait for the “perfect” season. Waiting for a better price is how limbs end up on shingles.

Permits are uncommon for a single residential tree on private property in most Upstate cities. HOA rules in Five Forks or newer Greer subdivisions can still require notice. Historic or protected trees are rare on typical lots. If you are unsure, ask your city or HOA before a large oak comes down.

The bottom line: budget $250–$700 for small trees and $700–$4,500 for most medium-to-large removals in Greenville. Complex or emergency work runs higher.
