Google Published Its Own Rules for AI Answers. Here's What Greenville, SC Businesses Should Do

Local Launch · Aug 2026 · 4 min read

Direct answer: Google now publishes an official guide to how websites get cited in AI answers — AI Overviews and AI Mode. The short version: AI pulls from the same search index Google has always used. The fundamentals that rank you in Google also get you cited by AI: a readable, fast site, clear business info, and content that says something real. Most Greenville-area businesses have the first two. Almost none have the third.

That third part is the gap, and it's fixable.

Google published "Optimizing your website for generative AI features on Google Search" on its own Search Central documentation. It's free, it's official, and it's written for website owners — not for SEO agencies. That's worth sitting down with.

What the guide says, in plain English

First: "Is SEO still relevant for generative AI search? In short, yes!" Google's AI features run on its regular search index. When AI answers your customer's question, it pulls from pages that already rank in Google. No mystery system. No secret sauce.

Second: Google calls the hype out. "GEO" and "AEO" — generative engine optimization, answer engine optimization — are its own words. From Google's view, optimizing for AI search is still SEO. Anyone selling you a "GEO-only" package is selling a new name for the same work.

What actually moves the needle, per Google

  • Content that says something real. Google's phrase is "non-commodity content." A first-hand story beats a summary of what everyone else wrote. The guide's own example: "7 Tips for First-Time Homebuyers" is commodity. "Why We Waived the Inspection & Saved Money" is not.
  • A clean technical base. Your pages have to be indexed and eligible for snippets. That means no duplicate junk, decent load speed, and a site that works on a phone.
  • Your Google Business Profile. Google says local business info can show up in AI responses. A thin or stale profile gives AI less to cite.
  • Images and video. AI features can bring in relevant images and video. Real photos of your work count.
  • Don't spam it. Google warns against building pages just to game AI answers. That's a spam policy violation, and it won't work long-term.

What that looks like for a Greenville trade

Take the guide's own example. Ask AI "how to fix a lawn full of weeds" and Google fans out to related questions: best herbicides, removing weeds without chemicals, preventing weeds. A Greenville landscaper with a page that actually answers those questions — with local specifics, not generic filler — gives AI something real to cite.

Same for a plumber with a page on what a water heater replacement actually costs in the Upstate. That's the kind of content that gets cited.
