Your Google Business Profile Is Not a Brochure Anymore

Local Launch · Aug 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer: if your Google Business Profile is set up and then left alone, it's slowly losing you customers in Greenville. In 2026, Google ranks the profiles that stay active, not the ones that look nice. Profiles with fresh posts, new photos, answered reviews, and accurate hours are beating older, bigger competitors in the map pack and in AI search results. The businesses winning are the ones feeding their profile on a schedule, not the ones who filled it out once.

Here's what we mean and what to do about it, plain and simple.

Google stopped rewarding a "set it and forget it" profile

For years, a Google Business Profile was a directory listing. Fill in your name, address, phone, add a few photos, done. That changed. Google now treats your profile like a live storefront, and it watches whether you keep it active.

The ranking signals are concrete. New reviews, and responses to them. Posts on a schedule. Fresh photos. Hours that are actually correct.

The numbers that move the needle

These are the benchmarks we track for our clients in the Upstate. They come straight from current local SEO reporting:

  • Reviews: aim for at least 5 new reviews a month. Busy niches like roofing and HVAC are pushing 10 to 15. Answer every one within 48 hours, including the bad ones.
  • Posts: one a week, minimum. Google gives Offers and Events posts more space than plain updates, so use those when you can.
  • Photos: upload new photos at least twice a month. Profiles with 100 or more photos drastically outperform profiles with fewer than 10.
  • Hours: accurate hours are now one of the biggest local ranking factors. Set holiday hours before the holiday. A wrong Saturday open time costs you real calls.
  • Q&A: add three to five questions and answers. Then answer any customer questions promptly.

These aren't guesses. They're the signals Google and the AI search engines are weighing right now.

What this looks like for a Greenville trade

Think of a Simpsonville landscaper and an Easley HVAC company. Both do good work. One posts a monthly special, answers reviews the day they come in, and uploads a photo of a finished patio each week. The other filled its profile out once in 2023 and never touched it again.

When a customer searches "landscaper near me," Google recommends the active one. When someone asks ChatGPT for a recommended plumber in Mauldin, the active profile is what gets cited. The work might be identical. The visibility is not.

The work is boring, but it compounds

None of this is complicated. That's the point. It's a 20-minute habit a week. Most Greenville-area businesses never start it, which is exactly why the ones who do are pulling away.
