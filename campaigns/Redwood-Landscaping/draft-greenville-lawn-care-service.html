Why Upstate Red Clay Changes the Game

Our soil here is red clay — Cecil sandy clay loam, to be exact. It holds nutrients well but compacts easily and drains slowly. That changes how you care for a lawn:

  • Timing beats volume. When you fertilize matters more than how much. A lawn care service that knows the Upstate schedules your feedings to the season, not to a generic national calendar.
  • Tall fescue and bermuda need different treatment. Most Upstate yards mix cool-season fescue (shade) with warm-season bermuda (sun). They need different mow heights and different feeding windows.
  • Aeration helps. Our clay compacts into a dense layer; core aeration opens it up for water and air. It's one of the best things you can do for a Greenville lawn.
