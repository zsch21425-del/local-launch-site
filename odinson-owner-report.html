🔴 Fix #1: Fix the broken business ID and missing contact info in your site's code Critical

The problem: Every website carries a hidden "business card" (schema markup) that tells Google and AI who you are. Yours has a machine-generated ID that reads like gibberish (a random GUID instead of your domain), the website URL is missing "https://", and the card contains no phone number, no state, and no ZIP code. When AI systems try to confirm "is this the same Odinson Electric on Yelp?" they get an incomplete picture.
The fix: Correct the business card to use your real identity: domain-based ID, full phone (864) 705-8494, complete address (12100 Pointe Grand Pl, Ste 12204, Simpsonville, SC 29680), and real hours. This is invisible to visitors but immediately makes Google and AI confident about who you are — a direct boost to map-pack and AI-recommendation visibility.

🔴 Fix #2: Make your business hours consistent everywhere Critical

The problem: Right now you tell the world different things about your hours in four places at once: your Google Business Profile and Yelp both say "Open 24 hours" every day; your site's llms.txt says "By appointment only" every day; and your site's hidden code says 9:00 AM–5:00 PM, Monday–Sunday. Google and AI cross-reference these sources — when they contradict, they get confused about whether you're open, may suppress you, or may show the wrong hours to a customer about to call.
The fix: Pick ONE set of true hours and make it identical in all four places: Google Business Profile, Yelp, your site's visible hours, and the hidden code. For a small operation where most work is by appointment, the honest answer (e.g., "Mon–Fri 8–6, on-call weekends") is fine — it just needs to match everywhere.

🟠 Fix #3: Create the AI knowledge file (llms-full.txt) High

The problem: AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) increasingly read a file called llms-full.txt to learn about a business. Your site has the short version (llms.txt — good!), but the full version returns a 404 not found. That's the difference between AI knowing "a few things about Odinson" and AI being able to accurately describe your services, service area, pricing philosophy, and owner story.
The fix: Add llms-full.txt with a rich, plain-English profile: company story, full service list with details (EV chargers, panel upgrades, rewiring, TV mounting, 12v–240v work), service area, FAQ answers, and contact info. This is the single highest-leverage GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) fix available — it's what makes AI recommend you when someone asks "who's a good electrician in Simpsonville?"

🟠 Fix #4: Speed up the site on phones — currently 51/100 High

The problem: A Lighthouse performance test scored your homepage 51/100 (large images, slow-loading first screen, too much background JavaScript). Google uses load speed as a ranking factor, and real homeowners on phones abandon slow sites. Your biggest sales asset — the project photos — is also your biggest weight.
The fix: Compress/optimize the large images (the hero uses a 2048px-wide Facebook-sourced image), lazy-load below-the-fold photos, and trim unused script. Keep the design exactly as-is — this is behind-the-scenes weight loss, not a redesign. Target: 80+ on mobile.

🟡 Fix #5: Fix accessibility issues — currently 74/100 Medium

The problem: The site scores 74/100 on accessibility: images without descriptive labels, low-contrast text, buttons without readable names, and headings that jump levels. Beyond being the right thing to do (and a legal consideration), Google's crawler uses these same signals to understand your content, and Lighthouse lowers your SEO score because of them.
The fix: Add descriptive alt-text to project photos (e.g., "200-amp panel replacement installed by Odinson Electric, Simpsonville SC"), fix contrast on small text, name the buttons, and restore proper heading order. Invisible to most visitors; measurable for search engines.

🟡 Fix #6: Sharpen the description shown in Google results Medium

The problem: The current meta description reads: "Looking for a reliable electrician? Our recommended Licensed electrician offers top-notch electrical repair services to meet all your needs." — it's generic template language that doesn't mention your name, your specialties (EV chargers, panel upgrades), or your area (Upstate SC). It's what people see under your link in Google, and it doesn't sell your real strengths.
The fix: Rewrite to something like: "Simpsonville SC's family-owned licensed electrician. Panel upgrades, EV charger installation, rewiring, lighting & TV mounting. BBB A- rated. Call (864) 705-8494." — specific, keyword-rich, and a real pitch.

🟡 Fix #7: Add structured FAQ + service data so AI quotes you correctly Medium

The problem: You have a genuinely good FAQ section on the site, but it's not marked up in the structured format AI and Google look for. When someone asks "does an electrician need a license in SC?" or "what does a panel upgrade cost?", Google and AI answer from structured data — without it, they may quote a competitor instead of you.
The fix: Tag the FAQ questions and answers in schema markup, and add per-service structured data (electrician, EV charger installation, TV mounting). Your content is already there — this just makes it machine-readable.

🟢 Fix #8: Update sitemap + robots so search engines crawl everything Low

The problem: Your sitemap lists only 2 pages (homepage + contract terms), and robots.txt doesn't point search engines to it. All your great service content lives on one page, which search engines read as thin — a single page can't rank for "EV charger installation Simpsonville" AND "panel upgrade Greenville" at the same time.
The fix: Make sure the sitemap includes every page and add the sitemap reference to robots.txt. Longer-term, dedicated service pages (one per specialty) unlock ranking for each of your services in different search areas — this is where the real search growth comes from.
